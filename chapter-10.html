The Chronicles of Renn — The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 10 — A Shadow at Dawn

By Lucas Montenegro

Three weeks after the events at the Stillroot Sanctuary, the Companion Ritual began beneath the oldest trees of Fyor.

Families gathered around a wide clearing. Lanterns hung from branches, and ribbons fluttered between the trunks. Young people stood near the center, some excited, others too nervous to speak.

Baranion waited beside Lyra.

“Still hoping for an Umbral Raven?” she asked.

He considered the question. “Not particularly.”

Lyra stared. “Who are you, and what have you done with Baranion Finrod?”

He smiled. “Very funny.”

“You've talked about ravens for years.”

“I know.”

“So what changed?”

Baranion watched two small foxes move through the undergrowth. “I think I cared too much about what a companion could make me.”

“And now?”

“I'd rather learn who it is.”

Lyra smiled. “That almost sounded wise.”

“Almost?”

“Don't get carried away.”

The ceremony began. One by one, young participants approached the waiting creatures. Some bonds formed quickly. Others did not.

A boy who had dreamed of a great hunting cat found himself chosen by a stubborn animal resembling a hedgehog. His expression made Lyra cover her mouth to hide her laughter.

A frightened girl was approached by a graceful silver-furred wolf. She knelt as it rested its head against her shoulder.

Then Lyra's turn came.

A small forest cat with sharp eyes and mottled fur stepped from the ferns. It circled her twice, sniffed her boots, and sat directly on her foot.

Lyra stared. “Really?”

The cat blinked.

She crouched and extended a hand. The creature pressed its forehead against her fingers. A smile spread across her face.

When she returned, the cat followed close behind.

“What are you calling it?” Baranion asked.

“Thistle.”

The animal tried to steal dried meat.

Baranion laughed. “That seems appropriate.”

Lyra retrieved the meat. “I think we're going to get along.”

When Baranion's turn came, he stepped into the clearing. He could feel his father's gaze from the crowd. Vhar sat on a branch above Elrohir's shoulder.

Baranion waited.

Several creatures watched from a distance. A small deer-like animal raised its head. A fox sniffed the air. Two ravens crossed the sky above.

None approached.

Minutes passed. Embarrassment rose in his chest.

He had imagined a raven descending from the canopy or a great beast emerging from the shadows.

Instead, nothing happened.

An elder stepped toward him. “There is no shame in waiting, young Finrod.”

Baranion looked around, then nodded. “I understand.”

To his surprise, he meant it.

He stepped back. Lyra greeted him with a sympathetic smile.

“I'm sorry.”

“Don't be.”

“Are you sure?”

Baranion watched Thistle trying to climb her sleeve. “I'm sure.”

Disappointment was not failure.

That evening, Baranion returned to the training platform. Sunset turned the bridges gold.

Elrohir approached quietly. “May I sit?”

Baranion nodded.

They watched the forest.

“You handled today well,” his father said.

“I didn't do anything.”

“Sometimes that's the difficult part.”

Baranion looked at him.

“You could have been angry,” Elrohir continued. “You could have demanded another chance.”

“I was disappointed.”

“That's different.”

Baranion considered that. “I suppose it is.”

Vhar landed on the railing, regarding Baranion with his usual expression of judgment.

“Don't even think about it,” the boy warned.

The raven dropped a small stone into his lap.

“Of course,” Baranion sighed.

Elrohir laughed. But when Baranion picked up the stone, he noticed a thin silver line running across its surface. A tiny black feather lay beneath it.

He looked at Vhar. The raven remained still, then flew toward the western forest.

“Father? Did Vhar ever return to the sanctuary?”

“Not that I know of.”

The feather might have belonged to any bird. Baranion slipped it into his pocket.

That night, Baranion dreamed of the forest. He stood beneath enormous trees, their branches stretching beyond sight. The air smelled of rain. A stream flowed over pale stones.

He followed it to a clearing where a white-barked tree stood beneath the moon.

Caelen Veyr sat among its roots, his face peaceful. A black raven perched beside him, its feathers gleaming in the moonlight. Beneath its feet lay a long, ordinary shadow.

Caelen smiled. “Thank you for finding him.”

Baranion felt something tighten in his chest. “Is he all right?”

Caelen looked toward the raven. The bird opened its silver eyes and spread its wings. It rose into the night sky. For a moment, its shadow crossed the ground beneath it. Then both bird and shadow disappeared among the stars.

“He was never meant to carry my fear forever,” Caelen said.

Baranion wanted to ask more, but the man faded. The clearing dissolved into mist, and Baranion woke.

Morning sunlight streamed through his window. The black feather rested on his bedside table. He picked it up and turned it between his fingers.

Perhaps the dream was real. Perhaps it was only what he wished to believe.

Either way, something inside him felt lighter.

Outside, Fyor was awakening. Bridges creaked, merchants shouted, children laughed. Somewhere above the rooftops, Vhar gave a familiar, irritating croak.

Baranion opened the window. “Good morning to you too!”

A stone struck the windowsill. He laughed.

Beyond the settlement lay the endless forest. Beyond it lay the vast world of Renn. There were mysteries waiting: old stories, forgotten places, creatures no one had ever seen.

Someday he would explore them, make mistakes, and perhaps find a companion who freely chose him.

Baranion turned toward the stairs. His father was waiting. Training would begin soon, and this time he intended to arrive early.

At least, that was the plan.

Far beyond the settlement, in the quiet clearing where the raven had been buried, sunlight filtered through the leaves. A smooth stone rested among the roots. Beside it lay a white flower.

A beetle climbed over the stone.

A raven's shadow passed across the grave.

There was no bird in the sky. No movement among the branches. Only sunlight. Only silence.

Then the shadow was gone.

And the forest continued to breathe.

THE END
