The Chronicles of Renn — The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 4 — The Missing Guardians

By Lucas Montenegro

Seris Aelwyn opened her door with ink on her cheek and a pencil behind one pointed ear.

“No,” she said.

Baranion blinked. “We haven't asked anything.”

“You look guilty, Lyra looks worried, and something is moving beneath your cloak. No.”

Lyra folded her arms. “Seris, it's important.”

“You said that about the whispering stone.”

“It did whisper.”

“It contained beetles.”

“Unusually loud beetles.”

Seris reached for the door, but a black head emerged from Baranion's cloak. Silver eyes met hers.

Her expression changed. “Where did you find that?”

Her study overflowed with books and maps. She cleared a table, opened the curtains, and watched the raven step into the sunlight.

The bird's bandage cast a shadow. Its body did not.

Seris moved a mirror beside it. The reflection appeared normally. She tried a polished plate and another mirror, then took a wooden tablet etched with fine lines from a cabinet.

“This detects disturbances around objects,” she said. “Stand back.”

Pale lines spread across the tablet. They stretched toward the bird, stopped, and bent downward as though something beneath the table were pulling them.

Lyra stepped away. “Is that normal?”

“No.”

The raven gave a harsh cry. A crack split the tablet.

Baranion winced. “Was that expensive?”

“Very.”

“Sorry.”

“Be sorry quietly.”

Seris examined the broken wood. “The lines weren't drawn to the bird. They were drawn toward something absent.”

“How can something absent pull on anything?” Lyra asked.

“An excellent question,” Seris replied. “I would rather not invent an answer.”

The raven turned toward the western window.

“The Hollow Vale lies that way,” Seris murmured.

“Where the patrol disappeared,” Baranion said.

Seris pulled down a volume describing the western concealment matrices. They had been rebuilt generations ago, and several older routes had been erased from modern maps.

“Erased?” Lyra asked.

“Not merely abandoned. Someone wanted them forgotten.”

Seris found a reference to an old sanctuary, but the page was damaged. She led them to the eastern archive, a vast chamber hollowed into an ancient tree.

Narrow bridges connected the upper shelves. Beneath the main collection, Seris unlocked a restricted room.

“Are we allowed in here?” Baranion whispered.

“I am.”

“That wasn't my question.”

“Then don't ask it.”

Inside a wrapped box lay two maps of the western forest. One showed familiar paths and boundary stones. The other included a narrow trail extending beyond the Hollow Vale. Thick black ink had been drawn across it.

“The Forgotten Path,” Seris read.

“Where does it lead?”

“To the Stillroot Sanctuary.”

Lyra frowned. “I've never heard of it.”

“Most people haven't.”

Seris unfolded another page. Only a few sentences survived the damage.

“The bond was not severed. The vessel endured, though the keeper was lost.”

Baranion felt the raven stir.

Seris continued: “Do not answer the voice beneath the roots.”

Silence filled the room.

Lyra looked at Baranion. “Tell her.”

He described the extinguished lamp and the whisper.

Find me.

Seris lowered the page. “I don't believe this bird found you by accident.”

They studied the maps until late afternoon. Seris identified several boundary stones along the old route, the first within a region normally considered safe under supervision.

“We should tell the guardians,” Lyra said.

“We will,” Seris replied. “But the search teams are already out there. Examining the damaged markers might help us understand what they're facing.”

Baranion thought of his father among shifting paths. “I want to help.”

Lyra sighed. “You always do.”

“Is that bad?”

“Only when you stop listening.”

Seris rolled the map. “We go only as far as the old boundary stones. If anything is wrong, we return.”

“Agreed,” Baranion said.

Lyra eyed him. “You agreed too quickly.”

“I can be reasonable.”

“Since when?”

As they left the archive, a messenger rushed past toward the western watchtower.

Baranion overheard two guardians speaking.

“Any word from Captain Finrod?”

“Not since the last horn.”

He felt his chest tighten. Lyra touched his arm.

“Your father knows the forest,” she said.

“I know.”

“And Vhar is with him.”

“I know that too.”

Behind them, the raven raised its head from Baranion's cloak. For an instant, its silver eyes seemed to reflect a tall figure standing at the end of the corridor.

Baranion turned.

The corridor was empty.

When he looked back, the bird had closed its eyes.
