The Chronicles of Renn — The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 9 — The Choice to Let Go

By Lucas Montenegro

The first tablet flared beside the pool. Then the second. Then the third.

Seris knelt at the edge of the ruined platform, tracing the surviving grooves with trembling fingers.

“The outer markings are responding!” she shouted.

The faceless figure twisted toward her. Darkness surged across the stones.

Elrohir stepped into its path. Vhar descended beside him, his enormous wings scattering the nearest shadows.

Lyra fired an arrow into the stone where a black hand was rising.

“Baranion! Move!”

Baranion held the raven close and retreated toward the edge of the platform. The bird weighed almost nothing. Its breathing was so faint he could barely feel it through his cloak.

“Stay with me,” he whispered.

The words sounded painfully familiar. He remembered Caelen saying them in the vision.

Baranion stopped.

That was the danger, wasn't it? Wanting so desperately for something to live that he stopped hearing what it needed.

He looked down at the raven. “I won't make you stay.”

The bird opened its silver eyes.

Seris pressed her hand against the final groove. A pale line raced through the platform. The faceless figure staggered, its robes dissolving into smoke for an instant.

Then it reached toward the raven. Shadows rose like waves. Elrohir struck through them, but they reformed around his blade.

“Seris!”

“One more moment!”

A black hand seized the edge of her cloak. Lyra rushed forward and cut the fabric free with her knife. Seris stumbled but kept her palm against the stone.

A sharp crack echoed through the clearing. One ancient groove split. Then another. The pool began to churn.

The faceless figure convulsed. Its empty face turned toward the raven. The darkness tightened as though something resisted being pulled apart.

Then a human face appeared.

Caelen.

Not the terrified man from the vision. Not the exhausted keeper who had begged the raven to survive. A man with sorrowful eyes.

He looked at the bird in Baranion's arms.

“Thank you,” he whispered.

The figure dissolved. Darkness collapsed into the pool.

Water rose in a silent column above the broken stones. For an instant, it reflected scattered memories: rain on leaves, a young man laughing, a raven spreading its wings, two companions walking beneath ancient trees.

Then the column fell. The stone beneath the pool split. The last markings went dark.

The sanctuary became silent.

Baranion sank to his knees. The raven lay still in his arms.

“Hey,” he whispered.

No response.

He touched its head gently. “It's over.”

The bird did not move. He waited, then waited longer.

Around him, the guardians lowered their weapons. Seris sat back on the stones, exhausted. Lyra approached and stopped when she saw the raven.

“Baranion…”

He shook his head. “Just a moment.”

She stayed beside him without speaking.

He had found the raven injured, carried it home, and promised to save it. Now he wondered whether he had failed.

A shadow fell across his hands. Elrohir knelt beside him.

“You gave it a choice,” his father said.

Baranion swallowed. “What if it chose wrong?”

“Then it was still its choice.”

A tear fell onto the black feathers. Baranion did not wipe it away.

They buried the raven beneath an ancient tree near the sanctuary. The guardians cleared a place among the roots. Lyra gathered white flowers from the clearing. Seris found a smooth stone and placed it at the head of the grave. Vhar perched silently above them.

Baranion knelt beside the earth. He had never given the raven a proper name. He had tried Ash and Silver. Neither seemed right. Perhaps Caelen had called it something different, a name lost with the records.

Baranion placed a fallen feather beside the stone.

“I wish I'd known you longer,” he whispered.

The wind stirred the leaves. For a moment, he imagined a soft croak. He looked up, but only Vhar watched from the branches.

Elrohir waited nearby.

“Ready?” his father asked.

“No.”

Elrohir nodded. “Neither am I.”

After a while, Baranion stood. “But we can go.”

The return journey was strangely ordinary. The Forgotten Path remained where it should. The trees no longer seemed to shift their distances. The boundary stones glowed steadily when Seris examined them.

At the western watchtower, a guardian announced that the concealment matrices had stabilized. The missing patrol members were recovering. Fyor was safe.

Baranion knew he should feel triumphant. Instead, he felt tired.

Lyra walked beside him along the upper platforms.

“You did the right thing,” she said.

“Did I?”

“I think so.”

“You're not sure?”

“No,” she admitted. “But I don't think anyone could be sure.”

He considered that and nodded. “I suppose that's fair.”

She nudged his shoulder. “You could try not running toward terrifying shadows next time.”

“I'll consider it.”

“That's not a promise.”

“No.”

Lyra sighed. “Some things never change.”

Baranion smiled.

Over the following days, the council examined the old records. Seris insisted that the story of Caelen Veyr and his raven be preserved rather than hidden again. Elrohir supported her. The sanctuary remained closed, but its history would no longer be erased.

Baranion helped copy the surviving documents. He was surprised by how little remained: a name, a drawing, a handful of warnings.

Caelen had loved his companion. That love had not been the mistake. The mistake had been believing that love gave him the right to decide how long the raven must endure.

One evening, Baranion returned to the storage room. The crate still stood beside the window. The water bowl was empty. He sat on the floor, remembering the bird's silver eyes.

Sunlight stretched across the wall. His shadow appeared beside him. He raised one arm; the shadow followed. He lowered it; the shadow did the same.

He laughed softly and opened the shutters.

The Companion Ritual was only days away. For years, he had imagined standing beside a great raven of his own. Now he wasn't sure what he wanted.

Perhaps that was all right.
