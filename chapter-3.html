The Chronicles of Renn — The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 3 — Whispers in the Dark

By Lucas Montenegro

Baranion checked the bandage before dinner. The raven's breathing had steadied, but its injured wing still hung awkwardly. Whenever he offered water, the bird drank and turned its gaze toward the western window.

“There's nothing there,” he told it.

The bird croaked.

“You disagree?”

He moved a lamp around the crate. The water bowl cast a shadow. The bandage cast a shadow. Even the scattered feathers did. The raven itself remained absent from the wall.

“That's impossible,” he said for perhaps the twentieth time.

The raven watched as though it had heard the observation before.

Elrohir returned after dark. Baranion sat at the dining table, attempting to look like someone who had spent an ordinary afternoon.

“How was your day?” his father asked.

“Normal.”

“Normal?”

“Very normal.”

Elrohir studied him. “Lyra visited?”

“Yes.”

“And the herbs were delivered?”

“Yes.”

“Without trouble?”

“Mostly.”

His father removed his cloak. “Several concealment matrices near the western boundary have shown disturbances. The guardians are investigating.”

“What kind of disturbances?”

“Markings losing their strength. Paths appearing where they shouldn't.”

Baranion looked toward the stairs.

“Until we understand the cause,” Elrohir continued, “stay away from the lower forest. And if you find anything unusual, tell me.”

“I will,” Baranion said.

The lie felt unpleasant. He left the table as soon as he could.

The storage room was quiet. Moonlight reached through the leaves, and the raven rested on its crate.

“My father says something's wrong near the western boundary,” Baranion told it. “Does that mean anything to you?”

The bird turned toward the window.

Baranion sighed. “You're probably just a very strange raven.”

The lamp flickered.

He frowned. There was no wind in the room.

The flame shortened, recovered, then flickered again.

Something moved on the wall.

Baranion saw the outline of his head and shoulders. His shadow. He stopped moving, but the shadow did not.

Slowly, its arm rose.

Baranion's real arms remained at his sides.

The dark fingers stretched toward the raven.

The bird cried out.

The lamp went dark.

Baranion stumbled backward into a crate. “Who's there?”

Only his breathing answered. Then two silver eyes opened in the darkness.

A voice rose around him, low and exhausted, as if it came from beneath the roots of the forest.

“Find me.”

The words did not come from the raven's beak. They seemed to emerge from the darkness itself.

“Who are you?” Baranion whispered.

A bell rang outside.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

The western alarm.

Footsteps thundered up the stairs. Elrohir opened the door with a lantern in one hand and his sword in the other.

“Baranion! What happened?”

“I—”

A distant horn sounded.

“Are you hurt?”

“No.”

“Did something attack you?”

“Not exactly.”

Elrohir noticed the raven on the crate. “Where did that bird come from?”

Before Baranion could answer, Vhar landed outside and gave an urgent cry. Elrohir's expression hardened.

“Stay inside,” he ordered.

“Father, I need to tell you—”

“Tell me when I return. We'll also discuss that bird.”

He disappeared downstairs.

Baranion watched from the window as guardians rushed along suspended walkways. Lanterns flared among the roots. Search lights moved through the lower forest. Something had breached the settlement's defenses.

Baranion barely slept. Whenever he closed his eyes, he saw his shadow lifting an arm. Whenever the wind rattled the shutters, he heard the voice again.

Find me.

By morning, rumors had spread throughout Fyor. A western concealment matrix had failed for several minutes. More troubling still, four guardians had disappeared near the Hollow Vale.

Lyra arrived with bread and an anxious expression.

“Your father joined the search,” she said. “He sent word before dawn.”

Baranion felt relief, but it was short-lived.

Lyra glanced at the storage room. “How's our mysterious friend?”

“There's something I need to tell you.”

He led her inside and described the lamp, the moving shadow, and the voice. She listened without interrupting.

“Did the raven speak?” she asked.

“I don't think so.”

“Then what did?”

Baranion looked at the empty space beneath the bird.

“I don't know.”

Lyra reached for her cloak.

“Where are you going?”

“To find someone who might.”

“Your mother?”

“No. Someone who studies things that shouldn't exist.”

She opened the door.

“We're going to see Seris.”
