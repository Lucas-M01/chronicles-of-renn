THE CHRONICLES OF RENN · The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter III — What the Shadow Kept

By Lucas Montenegro

Not every prison is built to hold a monster. Sometimes, it is built to keep one from escaping.

The raven was gone.

Baranion had checked the empty crate three times that morning, though he knew perfectly well the bird had been left behind at the Stillroot Sanctuary.

He had even caught himself reaching for the bowl of water beside it.

Now he sat on the edge of his bed, staring at the bandage scraps scattered across the floor.

Outside, Fyor was waking.

Merchants called to one another from the lower platforms. Wooden bridges creaked beneath passing footsteps. Somewhere above the rooftops, a flock of small birds erupted into noisy flight.

Everything sounded normal.

That was what troubled him most.

Somewhere beyond the western boundary, an injured raven was trapped beside a pool of black water.

And the settlement continued as though nothing had happened.

A sharp knock came at his door.

“Baranion.”

His father's voice.

“Come downstairs.”

The boy rose immediately.

Elrohir was waiting beside the dining table.

He had not changed out of his patrol clothes. His cloak hung over the back of a chair, and a fresh bandage covered his left forearm.

Vhar perched on the windowsill.

For once, the enormous raven did not throw anything.

That alone was enough to worry Baranion.

“Sit,” Elrohir said.

Baranion obeyed.

His father placed a folded piece of parchment on the table.

“Seris brought this to me before dawn.”

It was a drawing.

The same one Lyra had shown him the previous evening.

A man standing beside a raven.

Beneath them, an inscription.

The companion shall carry what the keeper can no longer bear.

Baranion looked up.

“Did she find out who they were?”

Elrohir nodded.

“The man's name was Caelen Veyr.”

“Was he a guardian?”

“Among other things.”

Elrohir sat across from him.

“Caelen lived here long before either of us was born. He helped maintain the western concealment matrices when the settlement was still much smaller.”

Baranion studied the drawing.

“And the raven?”

“His companion. According to the surviving records, they had been bonded for many years.”

“Then what happened?”

Elrohir's expression darkened.

“Something went wrong at the sanctuary.”

“What kind of something?”

“We don't know everything.”

Baranion looked down.

He had expected that answer.

It was the answer adults always gave when they wanted him to stop asking questions.

But Elrohir continued.

“The records describe an accident. Several people died. The sanctuary was abandoned, and the path leading to it was erased from our maps.”

“Why?”

“Because the people responsible feared that whatever happened there might happen again.”

Baranion frowned.

“That's not a reason to erase history.”

“No,” Elrohir said quietly. “It isn't.”

The boy looked up.

His father was staring at the parchment.

For a moment, he seemed older than Baranion remembered.

“Sometimes,” Elrohir continued, “people become so afraid of repeating a mistake that they decide no one should remember it.”

Baranion thought of the faceless man.

The black pool.

The raven screaming.

“Can we help them?”

Elrohir did not answer immediately.

Then he looked directly at his son.

“That's what we're going to find out.”

Seris had turned her study into a battlefield of paper.

Books lay open across the floor.

Old maps hung from the walls.

Several wooden tablets were arranged around a large table, each covered with carefully drawn patterns.

Lyra sat beside the window, sharpening an arrowhead.

She looked up when Baranion entered.

“You're allowed outside?”

“My father brought me.”

“Remarkable.”

“Don't start.”

“I wasn't going to.”

“You were smiling.”

“That's not a crime.”

Seris emerged from behind a stack of books.

“Good. You're here.”

She looked exhausted.

Her silver-blond hair had escaped its usual arrangement, and there was a fresh ink stain on her sleeve.

Baranion glanced at the scattered papers.

“Did you sleep?”

“Briefly.”

“How briefly?”

“That's irrelevant.”

Lyra leaned toward him.

“She didn't.”

Seris ignored her.

“I found three references to Caelen Veyr,” she said. “Two describe his work maintaining the old matrices. The third is an account written after the sanctuary was sealed.”

She placed a brittle page on the table.

“Unfortunately, much of it was deliberately damaged.”

Baranion leaned closer.

The surviving text was fragmented.

...the keeper refused separation...

...the shadow remained...

...the companion endured...

And, near the bottom:

...should the vessel perish, the remainder shall be released.

Baranion read the final line twice.

“What does that mean?”

Seris folded her arms.

“I believe the raven is holding something in place.”

“The faceless man?”

“Possibly. But I don't think the figure we saw is Caelen himself.”

Lyra stopped sharpening her arrow.

“Then what is it?”

Seris hesitated.

“Something left behind by him.”

Baranion remembered the reflection in the pool.

The man kneeling beside the raven.

The sorrow in his eyes.

“He was still there,” Baranion said.

Seris turned.

“What?”

“In the water. I saw him.”

“You saw a reflection.”

“I know what I saw.”

The room grew quiet.

Baranion looked at the drawing again.

“He was sorry.”

Lyra frowned.

“How do you know?”

The boy thought about the strange memory that had overwhelmed him at the sanctuary.

Cold stone.

Rain.

A gentle hand resting on black feathers.

Forgive me.

“I don't know,” he admitted. “I just do.”

Seris studied him.

Then she reached for another page.

“There is one more thing.”

She placed a diagram beside the drawing.

It showed two overlapping circles connected by a narrow series of markings.

One circle had been crossed out.

The other remained intact.

“This appears to describe part of the old ritual,” she explained. “Not enough to reconstruct it, thankfully. But enough to understand what its creator was attempting.”

“What was that?” Lyra asked.

Seris looked at the raven in the drawing.

“To prevent a bond from ending.”

The explanation came slowly.

Caelen Veyr had been a skilled guardian and a researcher of ancient magical traditions.

His raven had been gravely injured during an expedition beyond the western forest.

According to the surviving account, the creature was dying.

Caelen had searched for a way to save it.

Eventually, he discovered fragments of an old method of binding, one that interfered with the natural separation between two living beings.

It was not a method practiced by the people of Fyor.

Nor was it part of their traditional Companion Ritual.

Its origins were obscure, and the surviving records contained warnings rather than instructions.

Caelen had attempted it anyway.

“He wanted to save his companion,” Baranion murmured.

“Yes,” Seris said.

“And it worked?”

“For a time.”

She turned the page.

“The raven survived. But the ritual did not end as Caelen intended.”

“What happened?”

Seris looked at the diagram.

“I think he tried to take the creature's suffering into himself.”

Baranion remained silent.

“But whatever he drew from the raven did not disappear. It became entangled with something of his own.”

“His shadow,” Lyra said.

Seris nodded.

“Not an ordinary shadow. Something that took its shape.”

Baranion remembered the faceless figure.

“Then why doesn't the raven cast one?”

“Because the bond was never properly completed. The raven's shadow became the place where the failed ritual held its consequences.”

Lyra frowned.

“So the man in the pool…”

“Is what remained when Caelen lost control.”

“And Caelen?”

Seris lowered her eyes.

“The records say he died.”

Baranion looked toward the window.

For a long moment, nobody spoke.

Then he asked the question that had been troubling him since the previous night.

“If Caelen died, why is the raven still alive?”

Seris looked at him.

“That,” she said, “is what frightens me.”

The western boundary was heavily guarded when they arrived.

Elrohir had brought six experienced guardians, along with Seris, Lyra, and Baranion.

The boy had been surprised when his father allowed him to accompany them.

He had been even more surprised by the conditions.

He was not to leave Elrohir's sight.

He was not to approach the pool.

He was not to touch any unfamiliar markings.

And if Elrohir ordered him to retreat, he would obey immediately.

“No arguments?” his father asked.

“No arguments.”

Elrohir studied him.

“Baranion.”

The boy sighed.

“No arguments, Father.”

Vhar gave a satisfied croak from above.

Baranion glared at him.

“You're enjoying this.”

The raven clicked his beak.

Lyra hid a smile.

Seris carried a leather case filled with tablets, measuring instruments, and several small objects wrapped in cloth.

“What are those?” Baranion asked.

“Precautions.”

“Against what?”

“Things I would rather not discover firsthand.”

“That isn't comforting.”

“It wasn't intended to be.”

They crossed the boundary shortly after midday.

The forest beyond was unnaturally still.

The old path had become easier to follow, though Baranion could not explain why.

Leaves that had concealed the stones the previous day now lay scattered to either side.

Roots seemed to bend away from the trail.

And the farther they walked, the colder the air became.

One of the guardians stopped.

“Captain.”

Elrohir turned.

The man pointed toward a tree.

Its trunk bore a deep scratch.

Three parallel marks.

“That's ours,” the guardian said. “We marked this tree yesterday.”

Elrohir approached.

“Are you certain?”

“Yes.”

The guardian looked uneasy.

“It was beside the western boundary.”

Baranion stared at the tree.

They were nearly an hour beyond that boundary.

Lyra moved closer to him.

“The paths are changing again.”

Seris examined the markings.

“No,” she said.

Everyone turned.

“The paths aren't moving.”

She pointed toward the forest floor.

“The space between them is being distorted.”

Baranion looked around.

The trees seemed perfectly ordinary.

But when he focused on a distant trunk, he noticed something strange.

It appeared farther away than it should have been.

Then closer.

Then farther again.

As though the forest were breathing.

Elrohir drew his sword.

“Stay together.”

They reached the sanctuary under a sky that had begun to darken despite the afternoon sun.

The four ruined pillars stood around the circular platform.

The pool remained perfectly still.

And beside it lay the raven.

Baranion stopped.

The bird's injured wing was spread awkwardly across the stone.

Its feathers were dull.

Its breathing was shallow.

“It's still alive,” he whispered.

Elrohir placed a hand on his shoulder.

“Stay here.”

Seris approached the edge of the platform.

She knelt and removed a small tablet from her case.

The markings upon it glowed.

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then the water rippled.

A low sound rose from beneath the pool.

Like someone dragging a heavy object across stone.

One of the guardians raised his bow.

The raven opened its eyes.

Its gaze found Baranion immediately.

He felt a sudden pressure behind his ribs.

Not pain.

Something closer to grief.

The bird struggled to rise.

Its injured wing dragged across the platform.

“Don't move,” Baranion whispered.

The raven took another step.

Then collapsed.

He moved instinctively.

Elrohir tightened his grip.

“Baranion.”

The boy stopped.

His father looked at him.

“Remember what we agreed.”

Baranion swallowed.

Then nodded.

Seris placed the tablet on the ground.

A thin circle of light spread around the pool.

The water trembled.

And the faceless man emerged.

He rose slowly.

First his head.

Then his shoulders.

Then the long, dark robes that seemed to flow into the water beneath him.

His face was empty.

Smooth.

Featureless.

The guardians raised their weapons.

Elrohir stepped forward.

“Hold your positions.”

The figure turned toward him.

Then toward Baranion.

The boy felt the familiar chill.

His shadow stretched across the stones.

For a moment, it seemed to pull away from his feet.

Seris struck the ground with her palm.

The circle of light brightened.

Baranion's shadow snapped back into place.

“Don't look at it for too long!” she shouted.

The faceless figure raised one hand.

A guardian cried out.

His shadow rose from the ground, wrapping around his arm.

Another guardian seized him and pulled him backward.

Elrohir advanced.

Vhar descended from above.

The enormous raven struck the figure with his wings.

Dark feathers scattered.

Elrohir's sword followed.

The blade passed through the figure's chest.

For a heartbeat, the man dissolved into smoke.

Then reformed.

Lyra fired an arrow.

It struck the stone behind him.

The figure turned toward her.

Her shadow lengthened.

“Lyra!” Baranion shouted.

She jumped aside.

A black hand erupted from the ground where she had stood.

Seris gritted her teeth.

“This won't hold forever!”

The circle of light began to crack.

Elrohir retreated a step.

“Can you seal it?”

“Not while the old bond remains active!”

“Then how do we end it?”

Seris looked toward the raven.

Her expression changed.

Baranion saw it.

And understood.

“No.”

Seris turned toward him.

“Baranion—”

“No!”

The boy stepped forward.

“You're not killing it.”

Elrohir caught his arm.

“Listen to me.”

“No, Father! It hasn't done anything wrong!”

The faceless figure moved.

The circle shattered.

Darkness spilled across the platform.

One of the guardians fell.

Another shouted.

Vhar rose into the air, narrowly avoiding a black hand that reached from the stones.

Elrohir pushed Baranion behind him.

“RETREAT!”

The guardians began withdrawing.

Seris gathered her instruments.

Lyra seized Baranion's sleeve.

But the boy remained frozen.

Because the raven was looking at him.

And in its silver eyes, he saw something he had not noticed before.

Not fear.

Not pain.

Exhaustion.

The exhaustion of a creature that had been waiting for a very long time.

Baranion did not know why he did it.

Perhaps it was the memory of the raven lying injured beneath the fallen tree.

Perhaps it was the way the bird had watched him without trying to escape.

Or perhaps it was something his father had said during training.

A companion doesn't exist to fulfill your dreams.

He pulled free from Lyra's grasp.

“Baranion!”

He ran toward the platform.

Elrohir shouted.

The faceless figure turned.

Baranion's shadow rose from the ground.

He felt it seize his ankle.

He stumbled.

But instead of fighting, he reached toward the raven.

“I'm here!”

The bird lifted its head.

The darkness tightened around his leg.

Baranion fell to one knee.

He stretched out his hand.

“I'm not going to leave you!”

The raven moved.

Its beak touched his fingers.

And the world disappeared.

He stood beneath a gray sky.

Rain fell softly around him.

The sanctuary was whole.

Its pillars rose unbroken, and the pool reflected the clouds above.

A man knelt beside a black raven.

He was young, perhaps not much older than Elrohir.

His dark hair clung to his forehead.

His hands trembled.

The raven lay motionless in his lap.

Blood stained its feathers.

“Stay with me,” the man whispered.

The bird's breathing was faint.

“Please.”

Baranion tried to move.

His feet remained fixed.

The man lowered his forehead against the raven's.

“I promised I would bring you home.”

The scene shifted.

Night.

Candles burning around the pool.

Strange markings carved into the stone.

The man stood within the circle, holding the raven against his chest.

His voice shook as he spoke words Baranion could not understand.

The water darkened.

The candles went out.

Something moved beneath the surface.

The man screamed.

Then silence.

The scene changed again.

The sanctuary was ruined.

The man lay on the ground.

The raven stood beside him.

Its injured wing had healed.

But beneath its feet, there was no shadow.

The man raised a trembling hand.

His face was pale.

His eyes were filled with tears.

“I'm sorry.”

The raven pressed its head against his fingers.

The man's body began to fade.

Not into dust.

Into darkness.

His shadow stretched across the stones, rising slowly until it stood above him.

The figure had no face.

No eyes.

No mouth.

Yet it turned toward the raven.

The man looked at his companion one last time.

“Forgive me.”

Then he vanished.

The faceless figure remained.

And the raven was alone.

Baranion gasped.

He was back at the sanctuary.

The raven's beak still touched his fingers.

The faceless figure stood above him.

Its hand was raised.

But it had stopped moving.

Baranion stared at it.

He understood now.

Caelen had not simply tried to save his companion.

He had refused to accept that their bond might end.

And in doing so, he had created something neither of them could escape.

The raven had survived.

But it had been left carrying the consequences of its keeper's desperate choice.

For generations, it had remained bound to the sanctuary.

Waiting.

Perhaps for Caelen.

Perhaps for someone who could finally release it.

The faceless figure lowered its hand.

Baranion heard the voice again.

“Find me.”

This time, it was not a command.

It was a plea.

Baranion looked at the raven.

“You've been trying to find him.”

The bird blinked.

“But he's gone.”

The faceless figure trembled.

Its outline flickered.

For a brief instant, Baranion saw the man from the memory.

Caelen.

His face appeared beneath the darkness.

Then vanished.

The figure reached toward the raven.

Baranion shook his head.

“You can't bring him back.”

The darkness shuddered.

The shadows across the platform began to rise.

Behind him, Elrohir shouted something.

But Baranion barely heard.

He was watching the raven.

And he finally understood what it had been asking.

Not to be rescued.

Not to be claimed.

To be allowed to stop waiting.

“Seris!” Baranion shouted.

The elf looked toward him.

“Can you break the bond?”

She stared at the raven.

Then at the faceless figure.

“I don't know!”

“Can you try?”

“The old markings are damaged!”

“Then use what's left!”

Seris hesitated.

Elrohir moved beside her.

“Tell me what you need.”

She looked at the broken platform.

Then at the pool.

“The remaining markings are still connected to the sanctuary. If we can interrupt them in the correct sequence, the bond may collapse.”

“And the raven?” Elrohir asked.

Seris said nothing.

Baranion understood.

It might die.

The thought struck him harder than he expected.

He looked down at the bird.

Its silver eyes remained fixed on him.

For a moment, he wanted to pull it away.

To carry it back to Fyor.

To find another solution.

Perhaps there was a way to save it.

Perhaps, given enough time, someone could discover one.

But Caelen had thought the same thing.

And the raven had spent generations paying for that hope.

Baranion swallowed.

“It's your choice,” he whispered.

The raven tilted its head.

He placed his hand beside it.

“I won't force you.”

The bird looked toward the faceless man.

Then back at Baranion.

Slowly, painfully, it lowered its head against his palm.

Baranion closed his eyes.

“All right.”

Seris began with the outer markings.

She placed three small tablets around the pool.

Each one glowed with a pale light.

The remaining lines carved into the platform responded.

The faceless figure convulsed.

Darkness surged outward.

Elrohir stepped between it and Seris.

Vhar descended beside him.

The two moved together with the ease of countless years of shared battles.

Elrohir's sword struck the rising shadows.

Vhar swept through them, scattering the darkness long enough for the guardians to reposition.

Lyra fired arrow after arrow, forcing the shadows away from Seris.

“Baranion!” she shouted. “Get back!”

He lifted the raven carefully.

The bird was lighter than he expected.

Far too light.

Its breathing had become shallow.

He carried it toward the edge of the platform.

The faceless figure turned.

For the first time, its movements became frantic.

It reached toward the raven.

The shadows rose like waves.

Elrohir shouted.

“NOW, SERIS!”

She pressed both hands against the final tablet.

The markings blazed.

A sharp crack echoed through the clearing.

One of the ancient grooves split.

Then another.

The pool began to churn.

The faceless figure staggered.

Its body stretched, twisting toward the water.

Baranion held the raven close.

The bird opened its eyes.

A faint silver light reflected across its feathers.

Then the figure stopped struggling.

Its head turned toward them.

For one brief moment, a face appeared.

Caelen's face.

Not the frightened face from the ritual.

Not the exhausted face from the memory.

A face filled with sorrow.

He looked at the raven.

And smiled.

“Thank you.”

The words were barely audible.

Then the figure dissolved.

The darkness collapsed into the pool.

The water rose in a silent column.

For an instant, it reflected a thousand scattered images.

Trees.

Rain.

Stone pillars.

A young man laughing beside a great black bird.

Then the column fell.

The pool shattered.

Not the water.

The stone beneath it.

A deep crack split the platform from end to end.

The remaining markings went dark.

And the sanctuary fell silent.

Baranion remained on his knees.

The raven lay in his arms.

Its silver eyes were closed.

“Hey,” he whispered.

No response.

He gently touched its head.

“It's over.”

The bird did not move.

Baranion felt his throat tighten.

“Come on.”

He waited.

Nothing.

Around him, the guardians slowly lowered their weapons.

Seris sank to the ground, exhausted.

Lyra approached.

She stopped when she saw the raven.

“Baranion…”

He shook his head.

“Just a moment.”

The girl remained silent.

Baranion looked down at the creature.

He remembered finding it beneath the fallen tree.

The way it had watched him.

The way it had trusted him.

He had wanted to save it.

And now he wondered whether he had failed.

A shadow fell across his hands.

Baranion looked up.

Elrohir stood beside him.

His father knelt.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Elrohir placed a hand on his shoulder.

“You gave it a choice.”

Baranion swallowed.

“What if it chose wrong?”

Elrohir looked at the raven.

“Then it was still its choice.”

Baranion lowered his head.

A tear fell onto the bird's feathers.

He did not wipe it away.

They buried the raven beneath an ancient tree near the ruined sanctuary.

The guardians helped clear the roots.

Lyra gathered small white flowers from the edge of the clearing.

Seris placed a smooth stone beside the grave.

Elrohir stood nearby, Vhar perched silently on his shoulder.

Baranion said nothing.

He had never given the raven a name.

That troubled him more than he expected.

He had called it Ash.

Silver.

Raven.

But none of those names had truly belonged to it.

Perhaps it had once possessed a name.

Perhaps Caelen had spoken it every day.

Perhaps it had been forgotten along with the sanctuary.

Baranion knelt beside the grave.

“I wish I'd known you longer,” he whispered.

The wind moved through the branches.

He placed one of the raven's fallen feathers on the stone.

Then stood.

The others waited for him.

When he turned to leave, something caught his eye.

A dark shape beneath the tree.

Baranion stopped.

For a heartbeat, he thought it was another shadow moving on its own.

But it was only the outline of a branch swaying in the sunlight.

He let out a slow breath.

Then followed the others home.

The settlement of Fyor welcomed the returning guardians with relief.

Taren and the other survivors were recovering.

The western concealment matrices had stabilized.

The Hollow Vale no longer distorted the paths around it.

And the Stillroot Sanctuary had finally fallen silent.

The council ordered the area closed until the damaged structures could be examined.

But this time, the records would not be destroyed.

Seris insisted upon that.

Elrohir supported her.

And, to Baranion's surprise, several senior guardians agreed.

Whatever had happened at the sanctuary would be documented.

Not as a forbidden secret.

But as a warning.

Three weeks later, the Companion Ritual began.

The ceremony took place in a wide clearing beneath the oldest trees of Fyor.

Families gathered along the edges.

Young people stood in a circle, each awaiting the opportunity to approach a potential companion.

Some were nervous.

Others excited.

A few looked as though they might faint.

Baranion stood beside Lyra.

She nudged him.

“Still hoping for an Umbral Raven?”

He considered the question.

Then shook his head.

“Not particularly.”

Lyra stared at him.

“Who are you, and what have you done with Baranion Finrod?”

He smiled faintly.

“Very funny.”

“I'm serious. You've talked about ravens for years.”

“I know.”

“Then what changed?”

Baranion looked toward the trees.

A pair of small foxes moved through the undergrowth.

Farther away, a young deer-like creature watched the gathering with enormous amber eyes.

“I think I was more interested in what a companion could make me.”

Lyra studied him.

“And now?”

He shrugged.

“I'd rather find out who it is.”

Lyra smiled.

“That almost sounded wise.”

“Almost?”

“Don't get carried away.”

The ceremony began.

One by one, the young participants approached the animals.

Some bonds formed quickly.

Others did not.

A boy who had dreamed of bonding with a great hunting cat found himself chosen by a small, stubborn creature that resembled a hedgehog.

His expression was so astonished that Lyra nearly burst into laughter.

A girl who had feared the ceremony was approached by a graceful silver-furred wolf.

She knelt, trembling, as the animal rested its head against her shoulder.

Baranion watched quietly.

When his turn came, he stepped forward.

The forest seemed unusually still.

He waited.

Several creatures watched him from a distance.

None approached.

Minutes passed.

Then more.

The elders exchanged glances.

Baranion felt the familiar embarrassment rising in his chest.

He had imagined this moment countless times.

A raven descending from the canopy.

A great beast emerging from the shadows.

Something extraordinary.

Instead, nothing happened.

He lowered his eyes.

Perhaps he was not ready.

Perhaps no creature wished to bond with him.

He took a breath.

Then stepped back.

One of the elders approached.

“There is no shame in waiting, young Finrod.”

Baranion nodded.

“I understand.”

And to his own surprise, he meant it.

That evening, Baranion returned to the training platform.

The ceremony had ended hours earlier.

Lyra had successfully bonded with a small, sharp-eyed forest cat that seemed determined to ignore everyone except her.

She had named it Thistle.

Thistle had already stolen two pieces of meat and scratched a visiting guardian.

Lyra considered this a promising beginning.

Baranion sat on the platform's edge, watching the sunset.

Elrohir approached quietly.

“May I sit?”

Baranion nodded.

His father settled beside him.

For a while, they watched the forest in silence.

Then Elrohir spoke.

“You handled today well.”

Baranion shrugged.

“I didn't do anything.”

“Sometimes that's the difficult part.”

The boy looked at him.

Elrohir smiled.

“You were prepared to accept that no companion had chosen you.”

“I didn't have much choice.”

“You could have been angry.”

“I was disappointed.”

“That's different.”

Baranion considered that.

Then nodded.

“I suppose it is.”

Vhar landed on the railing beside them.

The enormous raven regarded Baranion with his usual expression of judgment.

“Don't even think about it,” the boy warned.

Vhar tilted his head.

Then dropped a small stone into Baranion's lap.

Baranion sighed.

“Of course.”

Elrohir laughed.

But when Baranion picked up the stone, he noticed something.

It was smooth.

Gray.

And marked with a thin silver line.

He turned it over.

A tiny black feather was caught beneath it.

Baranion froze.

He looked at Vhar.

The raven remained still.

Then it spread its wings and flew toward the western forest.

Baranion watched until it vanished among the trees.

“Father?”

“Yes?”

“Did Vhar ever go back to the sanctuary?”

Elrohir followed his gaze.

“Not that I know of.”

Baranion looked at the feather.

It was small.

Ordinary.

Perhaps it belonged to any number of birds.

He told himself that.

Then he slipped it carefully into his pocket.

That night, Baranion dreamed of the forest.

He stood beneath enormous trees, their branches stretching beyond sight.

The air smelled of rain.

Somewhere nearby, water flowed over stones.

He walked until he reached a clearing.

At its center stood a tree with pale bark.

Beneath it sat a man.

Caelen Veyr.

He looked younger than Baranion remembered.

His face was peaceful.

A black raven perched beside him.

Its feathers gleamed beneath the moonlight.

And beneath its feet lay a long, ordinary shadow.

Baranion stopped.

The man looked up.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Caelen smiled.

“Thank you for finding him.”

Baranion felt something tighten in his chest.

“Is he all right?”

Caelen looked toward the raven.

The bird opened its silver eyes.

Then spread its wings.

It rose into the night sky.

For a moment, its shadow crossed the ground beneath it.

Then both bird and shadow disappeared among the stars.

Caelen stood.

“He was never meant to carry my fear forever.”

Baranion wanted to ask a thousand questions.

But the man was already fading.

The clearing grew brighter.

The trees dissolved into mist.

And Baranion woke.

Morning sunlight streamed through his window.

For several minutes, he remained in bed, staring at the ceiling.

Then he sat up.

The small black feather lay on his bedside table.

He picked it up.

Turned it between his fingers.

And smiled.

He did not know whether the dream had been real.

He did not know whether Caelen and his companion had finally found peace.

Perhaps they had.

Perhaps his mind had simply given shape to what he wished to believe.

Either way, something inside him felt lighter.

Outside, Fyor was awakening.

The bridges creaked.

The merchants shouted.

Children laughed.

And somewhere above the rooftops, Vhar gave a familiar, irritating croak.

Baranion rose and opened the window.

“Good morning to you too!”

A stone struck the windowsill.

He laughed.

Then looked beyond the settlement.

Past the enormous trees.

Past the hidden paths.

Toward the endless forest that stretched beyond everything he knew.

There were still mysteries waiting in Fyor.

Old stories.

Forgotten places.

Creatures no one had ever seen.

And beyond the forest lay the vast world of Renn.

For the first time, Baranion did not feel the need to rush toward it.

He would explore it someday.

He would become stronger.

He would make mistakes.

And perhaps, when the time was right, he would find a companion willing to walk beside him.

Not because it was powerful.

Not because it could make him extraordinary.

But because they chose one another.

Baranion closed the window.

Then turned toward the stairs.

His father was waiting.

Training would begin soon.

And this time, he intended to arrive early.

At least, that was the plan.

Far beyond the settlement, in the quiet clearing where the raven had been buried, the morning sun filtered through the leaves.

A smooth stone rested among the roots.

Beside it lay a single white flower.

The wind stirred the grass.

A small beetle climbed over the stone.

And for one fleeting moment, a raven's shadow passed across the grave.

There was no bird in the sky.

No movement among the branches.

Only sunlight.

Only silence.

Then the shadow was gone.

And the forest continued to breathe.

End of Chapter III — What the Shadow Kept
