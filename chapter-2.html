The Chronicles of Renn — The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 2 — The Shadowless Raven

By Lucas Montenegro

The lower settlement smelled of medicinal smoke and wet bark. Baranion delivered the herbs to a caretaker tending a blue-striped fox, while Lyra waited beside the door.

“Tell your father we appreciate these,” the woman said. “Next time, perhaps before our supplies run out.”

“I'll relay the urgent message,” Baranion promised solemnly.

Outside, Lyra laughed. “Your heroic mission is complete.”

They were climbing toward the lifts when a weak cry rose from beneath the roots. It sounded like a raven, but the call broke midway, as if the creature had no strength left.

Baranion stopped.

“No,” Lyra said immediately.

“I haven't said anything.”

“You're about to leave the path.”

“Something is hurt.”

“Something might be luring us into the undergrowth.”

The cry came again. Baranion looked toward the boundary stone. Beyond it, ferns concealed a narrow passage.

“I'll only look.”

“Those are the words people say before a disaster,” Lyra muttered, following him.

The passage narrowed between immense roots. Black feathers clung to a thornbush. Farther on, beside a fallen tree, a raven lay on moss-covered stone. It was smaller than Vhar but far larger than an ordinary bird. One wing was twisted, and dried blood stained its feathers.

The raven opened its eyes.

They were silver: not dull gray, but bright as polished metal.

“Easy,” Baranion whispered, crouching. “We won't hurt you.”

Lyra kept a hand near her bow. “Be careful. Injured animals bite.”

The raven watched without struggling. Baranion loosened his cloak to wrap around its body. Then he noticed the sunlight.

A gap in the canopy illuminated the bird and the stone beneath it. Every leaf cast a shadow. Even the fallen feathers darkened the moss. Yet the raven itself cast nothing.

“Lyra. Look.”

She crouched and held her hand over the stone. Five clear shadows appeared beneath her fingers. She moved her hand beside the raven. The bird's body remained visible, but there was no shadow under it.

“That isn't possible,” she said.

Baranion moved a loose feather into the light. Its shadow appeared immediately.

“The feathers have shadows,” he said. “Only the bird doesn't.”

The raven lifted its head and gave a rough cry. For a moment, Baranion thought the sound contained his name.

“Did you hear that?” he asked.

“I heard a raven. What did you hear?”

“I don't know.”

Lyra wanted to take the creature to the caretaker. Baranion worried that the woman would call the guardians, who might remove the bird before they understood its condition.

“You can't keep every animal you find,” Lyra said.

“I'm not keeping it. I'm helping it.”

“Those are not always different things.”

Baranion looked down at the bird. Its breathing was shallow. “I can't leave it here.”

Lyra studied him, then helped support the injured wing. “Fine. But if it isn't improving tomorrow, we tell someone who knows what they're doing.”

“Agreed.”

“And don't try to bond with it.”

“I wasn't planning to.”

“You were thinking about it.”

“I was thinking about how heavy it is.”

“Then stop talking and carry it.”

They returned along the hidden passage. Baranion glanced back at the ferns. For an instant, the trees seemed darker than before, and he wondered how often he had passed this place without noticing anything strange.

Elrohir was still on patrol when they reached home. Baranion cleared a crate in the storage room beside his bedroom. Lyra brought clean cloth, water, and a small jar of salve. They cleaned the wound and bandaged the wing as carefully as they could.

The raven never struck at them. It simply watched with those unsettling silver eyes.

“Most wild birds would be fighting,” Lyra observed.

“Maybe it understands.”

“Or maybe it's exhausted.”

When they finished, the raven climbed onto the crate and folded its good wing. Baranion placed water beside it.

“You need a name,” he said.

“Don't name it,” Lyra warned.

“Ash?”

The bird looked away.

“Silver?”

It clicked its beak.

“Very helpful.”

Lyra paused at the door. “Remember. Tomorrow.”

After she left, Baranion opened the shutters. Evening sunlight stretched the crate's shadow across the floor. The water bowl cast a dark oval beside it. The raven remained shadowless.

He moved the lamp, watching shadows slide across the wall. Nothing changed beneath the bird.

“What happened to you?” he whispered.

The raven turned toward the western forest. Somewhere beyond the trees, an old concealment matrix began to fail.
