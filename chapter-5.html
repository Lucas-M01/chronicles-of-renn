The Chronicles of Renn — The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 5 — The Forgotten Path

By Lucas Montenegro

The western forest was unnaturally quiet. Usually, birds called among the branches and small animals moved beneath the ferns. That afternoon, even the insects seemed reluctant to make a sound.

Seris walked ahead with the old map. Lyra followed with her bow ready. Baranion carried the raven beneath his cloak.

“Last chance to turn back,” Lyra said.

“We haven't reached the stones yet.”

“I'm reminding you before you invent another reason to go farther.”

Seris raised a hand. They stopped beside a boundary stone whose markings had twisted as though the rock had briefly softened.

“These should be stable,” she murmured.

“Can you repair them?” Baranion asked.

“Not without the proper tools. Something is pulling against them from the other side.”

At the next marker, the trail seemed to end. Dense ferns concealed the ground, and a fallen trunk blocked the way. Lyra found the stone behind a curtain of vines. Someone had deliberately carved away its markings.

“Why would anyone do this?” she asked.

“To hide what lay beyond,” Seris replied.

The raven stirred and pointed its beak toward a gap between the roots. A breeze moved through the trees. Dry leaves slid aside, revealing a row of flat stones.

“The path,” Baranion whispered.

Lyra folded her arms. “We agreed to stop at the markers.”

Before Seris could answer, a weak human cry sounded from farther west.

Baranion turned. “Someone's hurt.”

The cry came again, followed by silence.

Lyra let out a breath. “Fine. We follow the sound, not the bird. And we turn back at the first sign of danger.”

The Forgotten Path twisted beneath enormous roots. Pale trunks leaned over it, and fused branches formed arches overhead. Their footsteps sounded strangely distant.

Baranion looked back and could no longer see the marker they had passed only moments earlier.

“Are we going in circles?” he asked.

Seris examined the map. “I don't think so.”

“That isn't reassuring,” Lyra said.

The air smelled of rain, though the sky above the canopy was clear. The raven grew restless beneath Baranion's cloak.

“Easy,” he whispered. “We're almost there.”

He did not know how he knew.

The trees opened around a ruined sanctuary. Four pillars surrounded a circular platform. Three had fallen; the fourth was covered in vines. At the center lay a shallow pool whose water reflected the sky without a ripple.

No insects crossed its surface. No leaves floated there.

Lyra drew her bow. “We should leave.”

Baranion agreed. Then the raven struggled free, landed awkwardly, and hopped toward the pool, dragging its injured wing.

“Wait!”

He followed it to the water's edge and looked down.

His reflection stared back. Beside it stood the raven, its injured wing whole.

Behind them stood a man in dark robes.

Where his face should have been, there was nothing.

Baranion spun around. No one stood behind him.

He looked into the pool again. The figure remained.

“Lyra!”

She hurried over. “What?”

“There's someone in the reflection!”

She looked down. The figure had vanished.

Seris knelt beside the pool. “Don't touch it.”

She brushed moss from the platform, revealing carved patterns and a deep circular groove.

“This was a ritual site,” she said. “And something here is still active.”

She uncovered an inscription and read: “One life offered. One life preserved. One shadow left behind.”

The raven trembled.

A branch snapped behind them. Lyra spun, raising her bow.

A guardian staggered from the trees. His cloak was torn, blood stained his temple, and one arm hung uselessly at his side. He collapsed to his knees.

“We're from Fyor,” Lyra said. “Who are you?”

“Taren,” he whispered. “Western patrol.”

Seris knelt beside him. “You're one of the missing guardians. Where are the others?”

Taren looked past them toward the sanctuary. Then he saw the raven.

“No,” he whispered.

His face drained of color.

“That thing was there when the others disappeared.”

They helped Taren to the edge of the clearing. He drank water with trembling hands.

“We followed a voice,” he said. “It sounded like one of our own. It knew our names.”

Baranion felt cold. “What happened?”

“We saw a man between the trees. He had no face. One of us drew his sword, and then his shadow stood up.”

No one spoke.

“It rose from the ground and grabbed him. The paths changed. I ran. I don't know where the others went.”

Lyra fired a signal arrow. Green sparks burst above the canopy, and a distant horn answered.

Help was coming.

Baranion looked toward the raven. A dark shape stretched across the pool beneath its reflection. For the first time since he had found it, the bird appeared to have a shadow.

But the shadow was not beneath its feet.

It was moving toward the center of the water.

And something beneath the surface began to rise.
