THE CHRONICLES OF RENN · The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 1 — An Unusual Morning

By Lucas Montenegro

In the ancient Forest of Fyor, even an ordinary morning could bring unexpected trouble.

The first sign that it was going to be a terrible day was the raven.

Not the raven itself, but what it dropped.

A small, smooth stone struck Baranion Finrod squarely on the forehead, waking him with a startled cry.

“Ouch!”

He sat up so quickly that his head collided with the wooden beam above his bed.

Outside the window, an enormous black raven watched him with its head tilted to one side. Its golden eyes seemed to gleam with satisfaction.

“I hate that bird,” Baranion muttered, rubbing his forehead.

The raven croaked.

“And you know it!”

Another croak.

“Don't pretend you don't understand!”

The bird spread its massive wings and disappeared among the branches.

Its name was Vhar, and it was his father's companion.

Baranion had known him for as long as he could remember. Unfortunately, the raven seemed to consider tormenting him every morning a personal responsibility.

“Baranion!” a voice called from downstairs. “If you're arguing with Vhar again, you should know he's not going to lose.”

“He started it!”

“He's a raven!”

“A very ill-mannered raven!”

Elrohir Finrod's laughter echoed through the house.

Baranion sighed.

The morning had barely begun.

The hidden settlement of Fyor was slowly awakening.

Built among trees so ancient that their roots seemed to belong to another age, the settlement was a maze of wooden platforms, suspended bridges, and houses constructed around enormous trunks.

Some homes stood so high that their inhabitants could look out their windows and see nothing but clouds drifting beneath the branches.

Others remained hidden among thick foliage, surrounded by hanging gardens and lanterns of colored glass.

Sunlight filtered through the canopy, illuminating the walkways as merchants prepared their stalls and children hurried between houses.

The smell of freshly baked bread mingled with the damp fragrance of the forest.

Baranion loved these mornings.

There was something comforting about the way the settlement came to life, as though nothing existed beyond the trees.

But he knew better.

Beyond Fyor's hidden borders lay kingdoms, empires, and distant lands that he had only heard about in stories.

His father had seen some of them.

Baranion dreamed of seeing them too.

“Late again?”

The familiar voice interrupted his thoughts.

Lyra Vaelis stood beside a bridge railing, her copper-colored hair tied in a braid. A bow rested across her back, and a small leather pouch hung from her belt.

“I'm not late,” Baranion replied.

“Training started fifteen minutes ago.”

“That's a matter of interpretation.”

“Fifteen minutes?”

“Of the concept of punctuality.”

Lyra raised an eyebrow.

“Your father's waiting.”

Baranion grimaced.

“How angry does he look?”

“He's smiling.”

“That's worse.”

Lyra nodded solemnly.

“Much worse.”

The training ground occupied a circular platform built around a gigantic tree.

Young half-elves practiced with wooden swords, bows, and staffs. Some trained alongside magical creatures, preparing for the responsibilities they would eventually undertake.

Baranion spotted a boy struggling to command a six-legged feline.

“Sit!” the boy ordered.

The creature yawned.

“Sit!”

It rolled onto its back.

Baranion smiled.

“At least someone's having a worse morning than I am.”

“Baranion.”

His father's voice wiped the smile from his face.

Elrohir stood at the center of the platform, holding a wooden sword.

Tall and broad-shouldered, with dark hair tied behind his head and a faint scar crossing his left eyebrow, he looked every bit the experienced guardian he was.

Unfortunately, he was also smiling.

“Sleep well?” Elrohir asked.

“Very well.”

“Good. Vhar told me you needed your rest.”

“Vhar can't talk.”

“Not to you.”

Several nearby trainees laughed.

Elrohir tossed him a wooden sword.

“Let's begin.”

Baranion caught it.

“No warm-up?”

“You've already had fifteen minutes.”

“I was walking here!”

“Then you're warmed up.”

Lyra stepped aside, barely concealing her amusement.

Baranion took his stance.

His father did the same.

For a moment, neither moved.

Then Baranion lunged.

His first strike was fast, aimed at Elrohir's shoulder.

His father dodged effortlessly.

Baranion followed with a second strike, then spun toward his father's unguarded side.

A wooden blade touched his ribs.

“Dead,” Elrohir announced.

“I was still attacking!”

“Dead while attacking.”

Baranion stepped back.

“Again.”

This time, he attempted a more elaborate combination.

Two high strikes.

A short retreat.

A sudden thrust.

Elrohir avoided the final attack and placed the tip of his sword against Baranion's throat.

“Dead again.”

“You could at least pretend I'm giving you trouble.”

“I could. But that would make for terrible training.”

Baranion lowered his weapon.

“I'm never going to beat you.”

“Not with that attitude.”

His father studied him for a moment.

“You're thinking about the ritual.”

Baranion looked away.

Only a few weeks remained until the Companion Ritual.

For generations, the people of Fyor had formed bonds with magical creatures through the ceremony. Some found small companions. Others bonded with powerful beasts that remained beside them for decades.

Baranion had always dreamed of bonding with an Umbral Raven.

The enormous birds possessed extraordinary senses, remarkable intelligence, and a natural affinity for shadows.

His father and Vhar were renowned for their patrols through the most dangerous regions of Fyor.

Baranion wanted to follow in their footsteps.

“Do you think I'll manage it?” he asked.

“Manage what?”

“Bonding with an Umbral Raven.”

“Perhaps.”

“Just perhaps?”

Elrohir rested his sword against his shoulder.

“The ritual isn't a marketplace, Baranion. You don't choose a companion the way you choose a weapon.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

Baranion remained silent.

“A companion has its own instincts, fears, and desires,” Elrohir continued. “A bond requires more than strength. It requires trust.”

“I understand.”

“Then stop thinking only about what you want to receive.”

Baranion considered his father's words.

“And think about what I have to offer?”

Elrohir smiled.

“Now you're beginning to understand.”

A loud croak came from above.

Vhar sat on a branch, watching them.

Baranion pointed at him.

“I'd offer him the opportunity to learn some manners.”

The raven dropped another stone.

Elrohir turned away to hide his laughter.

By the time training ended, Baranion's arms ached and his pride had suffered considerably.

He was gathering his belongings when Elrohir approached.

“I have a task for you.”

Baranion immediately straightened.

“A patrol?”

“No.”

“Scouting?”

“No.”

“Something involving a sword?”

“Medicinal herbs.”

Baranion stared at him.

His father handed him a small wrapped bundle.

“Take these to the animal caretaker in the lower settlement.”

“Herbs?”

“Important herbs.”

“Couldn't someone else do it?”

“Certainly.”

Elrohir smiled.

“But I'm asking you.”

Baranion accepted the package with a defeated sigh.

As he turned to leave, his father spoke again.

“And stay on the marked paths.”

“I know, Father.”

“The lower forest isn't a place for wandering.”

“I know.”

Elrohir placed a hand on his shoulder.

“Good.”

Baranion headed toward the stairs, where Lyra was already waiting.

“What did he give you?” she asked.

He held up the bundle.

“An incredibly dangerous mission.”

Lyra inspected it.

“Herbs?”

“Don't underestimate them.”

She laughed.

Together, they descended toward the lower platforms, unaware that the quiet afternoon ahead would change far more than Baranion's plans for the Companion Ritual.

Somewhere beyond the marked paths, deep among the roots of the ancient forest, a wounded raven opened its silver eyes.

And waited.

End of Chapter 1 — An Unusual Morning
