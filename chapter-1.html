The Chronicles of Renn — The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 1 — An Unusual Morning

By Lucas Montenegro

The first sign that it was going to be a terrible day was the raven. Not the raven itself, but what it dropped. A smooth gray stone struck Baranion Finrod on the forehead, waking him with a cry. He sat up too quickly and struck the beam above his bed.

Outside the window, an enormous black raven watched with golden eyes. “I hate that bird,” Baranion muttered. The bird croaked. “And you know it!”

Its name was Vhar, and it was his father's companion. Vhar had apparently made tormenting Baranion a personal responsibility.

“Baranion!” Elrohir called from downstairs. “If you're arguing with Vhar, you should know he won't lose.”

“He started it!”

“He's a raven!”

“A very ill-mannered raven!”

Elrohir's laughter followed him down the stairs.

Fyor awakened slowly around them. Houses clung to the trunks of enormous trees, linked by suspended bridges and narrow wooden platforms. Some homes rose so high that clouds drifted beneath their windows. Others hid among hanging gardens and leaves, their colored lanterns still glowing in the early light. The scent of fresh bread mingled with damp bark.

Baranion loved the settlement, but he dreamed of the lands beyond its concealed boundaries. His father had seen kingdoms and cities that Baranion knew only from stories. One day, he meant to see them too.

“Late again?” Lyra Vaelis asked from a bridge railing. Her copper hair was braided, and her bow rested across her back.

“I'm not late.”

“Training started fifteen minutes ago.”

“That's a matter of interpretation.”

“Your father is smiling,” Lyra said.

Baranion groaned. “That's worse.”

The training platform encircled an immense tree. Young half-elves practiced with bows and wooden swords; others trained beside magical animals. One boy tried to command a six-legged feline. The creature yawned and lay down.

“At least someone's having a worse morning,” Baranion whispered.

“Baranion,” said Elrohir.

His father stood in the center, dark hair tied back, a faint scar crossing one eyebrow. He tossed Baranion a wooden sword.

“No warm-up?” Baranion protested.

“You had fifteen minutes.”

“I was walking here!”

“Then you're warmed up.”

Baranion lunged. His father dodged, tapped his ribs with the wooden blade, and said, “Dead.”

“I was still attacking!”

“Dead while attacking.”

On the second attempt, Baranion feinted high and thrust low. Elrohir's sword rested against his throat before he could finish.

“Dead again.”

“You could pretend I'm difficult to defeat.”

“That would be poor training.”

Baranion lowered his sword. “I'll never beat you.”

“Not with that attitude.” Elrohir studied him. “You're thinking about the Companion Ritual.”

Only weeks remained. For generations, Fyor's young people had formed bonds with magical creatures. Some found small companions; others formed lifelong partnerships with great beasts. Baranion wanted an Umbral Raven like Vhar, a creature of extraordinary intelligence and keen senses. He imagined patrolling the forest beside it, as his father did.

“Do you think I'll bond with one?” he asked.

“Perhaps.”

“Just perhaps?”

“The ritual isn't a marketplace,” Elrohir said. “A companion has desires and fears of its own. A bond requires trust, not merely strength.”

“I know.”

“Then consider what you have to offer, rather than what you hope to gain.”

Baranion thought about that. “I suppose you're right.”

Vhar croaked overhead.

“I'd offer him a lesson in manners,” Baranion added.

The raven dropped another stone. Elrohir turned away to hide his laughter.

After training, Elrohir handed Baranion a wrapped bundle.

“A mission?” Baranion asked eagerly.

“Deliver these medicinal herbs to the animal caretaker in the lower settlement.”

“Herbs?”

“Important herbs.”

Baranion accepted them with a sigh.

“Stay on the marked paths,” his father added. “The lower forest isn't a place for wandering.”

“I know, Father.”

Lyra was waiting at the stairs.

“What did he give you?”

“An incredibly dangerous assignment.”

She inspected the package. “Herbs?”

“Don't underestimate them.”

They descended through the platforms together, joking as they went. Neither knew that the afternoon would change everything Baranion believed about the Companion Ritual.

Far beneath the trees, beyond the marked paths, a wounded raven opened its silver eyes and waited. It had waited through the passing of countless seasons, though Baranion could not yet imagine such a thing. That afternoon, the forest would bring their paths together.
