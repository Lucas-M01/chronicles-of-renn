The Chronicles of Renn — The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 8 — The Keeper's Shadow

By Lucas Montenegro

The guardians crossed the western boundary shortly after midday. Elrohir led six experienced patrol members. Seris carried a case of tablets and measuring instruments. Lyra kept her bow ready, and Baranion walked close to his father.

Above them, Vhar moved between the branches.

The forest grew colder as they approached the Forgotten Path. A guardian stopped beside a tree marked with three deep scratches.

“We passed this yesterday,” he said.

“Are you certain?” Elrohir asked.

“Yes, Captain. Near the boundary.”

They had been walking for nearly an hour.

Seris examined the trunk. “The paths aren't moving. The distances between them are changing.”

Lyra glanced at Baranion. “That's somehow worse.”

Elrohir drew his sword. “Stay together.”

The Stillroot Sanctuary stood beneath a darkening sky. The pillars remained ruined, and the pool was perfectly still.

Beside it lay the raven. Its injured wing spread across the stone, its breathing faint.

Baranion stopped. “It's alive.”

Elrohir placed a hand on his shoulder. “Stay here.”

Seris approached the platform and placed a tablet beside the pool. Pale lines spread across the stone. The water rippled.

The raven opened its silver eyes.

Then the faceless figure rose.

“Hold your positions,” Elrohir ordered.

The figure lifted a hand. A guardian shouted as his shadow wrapped around his arm. Another pulled him back.

Elrohir charged. Vhar descended with a thunderous beat of wings, scattering darkness long enough for the guardians to retreat.

Lyra fired an arrow. It passed through the figure's chest.

Seris pressed her hands against the platform. A ring of light formed around the pool.

“This won't hold forever!” she shouted.

The figure turned toward Baranion. His shadow lengthened.

Then the raven cried out. Not a warning, but a sound of pain.

Baranion realized the bird was not afraid of the faceless figure. It was afraid of what might happen if the figure reached him.

The ring of light cracked.

“Retreat!” Elrohir shouted.

Lyra grabbed Baranion's sleeve. But the raven struggled to lift its head, and he remembered his father's words.

A companion doesn't exist to fulfill your dreams.

He pulled free and ran toward the platform.

“Baranion!”

His shadow rose around his ankle. He fell to one knee but stretched out his hand.

“I'm here!”

The raven moved toward him. Its beak touched his fingers.

The world vanished.

Rain fell beneath a gray sky. The sanctuary was whole, its pillars unbroken around a pool reflecting clouds.

A young man knelt on the stones, holding a black raven against his chest. Blood stained the bird's feathers.

“Stay with me,” the man whispered.

Baranion recognized Caelen Veyr from the drawing.

“I promised I'd bring you home,” Caelen said.

The scene shifted.

Night surrounded the sanctuary. Candles burned around the pool. Strange markings covered the stone. Caelen stood inside a circle with the raven held close, speaking words Baranion could not understand.

The pool darkened. The candles went out. Something moved beneath the water.

Caelen screamed.

The vision changed again. The sanctuary lay in ruins. Caelen had fallen beside the pool. The raven stood near him, alive, its injured wing healed.

But it cast no shadow.

Caelen raised a trembling hand. “I'm sorry.”

The bird pressed its head against his fingers.

Darkness gathered beneath the man. His shadow stretched across the stones, then rose slowly into the shape of a standing figure. It had no face, no eyes, no mouth.

Caelen looked at his companion one last time.

“Forgive me.”

His body faded. The faceless figure remained.

And the raven was alone.

Baranion gasped. He was back at the ruined sanctuary. The raven's beak still rested against his hand. The faceless figure stood above him, its raised hand motionless.

Caelen had tried to save the creature he loved. But he could not accept the possibility of losing it. His desperate attempt had preserved the raven's life while trapping it beside the consequences of his choice.

The faceless figure was not Caelen returned. It was what he had left behind.

The raven had endured for generations, waiting for a keeper who could never truly come back.

“Find me,” the voice whispered.

This time, Baranion heard the grief beneath the words.

“You've been trying to find him,” he told the raven. “But he's gone.”

The figure trembled. For an instant, Caelen's face appeared beneath the darkness. Then it vanished.

“You can't bring him back,” Baranion whispered.

Behind him, Elrohir shouted, but Baranion understood. The raven was not asking for a new keeper. It was asking to let the old bond end.

“Seris!” he shouted. “Can you break it?”

“I don't know!”

“Can you try?”

“The markings are damaged!”

“Then use what's left!”

Seris studied the platform and opened her case. Elrohir stepped beside her.

“What do you need?”

“Time,” she said. “And everyone away from the pool.”

Lyra drew another arrow. “Time we can try to give you.”

Baranion looked down at the raven. If the bond ended, it might not survive. He wanted to promise that everything would be all right, but he could not.

Instead, he placed his hand beside it.

“It's your choice,” he whispered.

The raven lowered its head against his palm.

Baranion closed his eyes. “All right.”

He lifted the bird carefully.

Behind him, Seris began to work, and the faceless figure screamed without a mouth.
