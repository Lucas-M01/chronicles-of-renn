The Chronicles of Renn — The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 6 — The Faceless Man

By Lucas Montenegro

The water darkened without making a sound. A shadow stretched across its surface, reaching from the raven's reflection toward the center.

Then a voice rose from the depths.

“Find me.”

Baranion staggered. Seris turned sharply.

“You heard that?”

He nodded.

The voice came again, weaker. “Please.”

A hand emerged from the black water, followed by an arm and the outline of a man in dark robes. He rose without disturbing the surface. His face was smooth and empty.

Lyra raised her bow. “Get away from the pool!”

The figure turned toward the raven. The bird gave a terrible cry and collapsed.

Baranion ran forward. “Stop!”

A shadow snapped around his ankle. He fell against the stone. His own shadow had risen from the platform and wrapped itself around his leg.

He pulled, but it tightened. More dark hands pushed through the stone.

“Baranion!” Lyra shouted.

An arrow struck the platform beside the figure. Seris dropped to one knee and pressed both hands against the carved surface. Pale lines flashed around the pool.

The shadows loosened.

“Now!” Seris cried.

Baranion tore himself free. Lyra grabbed his collar and dragged him back.

A vast black shape swept through the clearing.

Vhar.

The enormous raven struck the figure with both wings, scattering darkness like smoke. Elrohir landed on the platform a heartbeat later, sword drawn.

“BARANION!”

“Father!”

Several guardians emerged from the trees. Elrohir stood between the children and the pool. The faceless figure began to reform.

Elrohir noticed the injured raven. His expression changed.

“Everyone away from the water.”

The guardians formed a line. Seris helped Taren to his feet. Lyra pulled Baranion toward the trees, but he resisted.

“The raven!”

“Leave it!” Elrohir ordered.

“We can't!”

The figure raised one hand. Shadows beneath the guardians twisted. Elrohir struck through them with his sword while Vhar descended again.

“GO!”

Lyra pulled harder. Baranion stumbled.

Then the raven looked at him.

Their eyes met.

Cold stone beneath bare feet. Rain against skin. A hand resting gently on black feathers. A man's voice, warm and exhausted.

Forgive me.

The sensation vanished. Baranion gasped.

Across the platform, a shadow stretched from the raven's body toward the faceless figure. It moved as though the man and bird were parts of the same thing.

Elrohir was the last to retreat. At the boundary stones, he pressed a carved object into the earth. Pale light spread between the trees, and the sanctuary disappeared behind a shimmering barrier.

The figure did not follow. Neither did the raven.

Baranion stood beside Lyra, breathing hard. His father approached.

“What were you thinking?”

“I wanted to help.”

“You entered a restricted part of the forest. You put Lyra and Seris in danger. You nearly died.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

Baranion told him everything: the injured bird, the missing shadow, the voice in the storage room, the map, and the Forgotten Path.

Elrohir listened without interrupting.

“You should have come to me,” he said at last.

“I tried last night.”

Elrohir looked at him.

“But I should have told you sooner,” Baranion admitted.

“Yes.” His father sighed. “You should have.”

They stood silently for a moment. Then Elrohir placed a hand on his shoulder.

“I'm glad you're alive.”

“So am I.”

They returned to Fyor before sunset. Taren was taken to the healers, and the western boundary was placed under guard. Search parties found the other missing guardians near an old ravine, injured and frightened but alive.

Baranion should have felt relieved. He was, but he could not stop thinking about the raven.

He had carried it out of the forest because it was hurt.

Now he had left it behind.

That evening, Lyra visited with bread and a folded sheet of paper.

“Seris found something.”

Baranion sat up. “What?”

“An old drawing.”

It showed a man standing beside a raven. The man's face was damaged by age, but the bird was unmistakable: black feathers, silver eyes, and a long shadow beneath its feet.

“Is it the same raven?” Baranion asked.

“Seris thinks so.”

“How old is this?”

“Very.”

Lyra pointed to the inscription: The companion shall carry what the keeper can no longer bear.

Baranion read it twice.

“What does that mean?”

“She doesn't know yet. But she doesn't think the raven lost its shadow.”

“Then where is it?”

Lyra hesitated. “She thinks someone took it.”

Beyond the barrier, the sanctuary stood silent. The raven lay beside the pool, breathing shallowly.

A pale, scarred hand emerged from the black water and reached toward it. The bird opened its silver eyes and touched the fingers with its beak.

For a moment, the water reflected a man kneeling beside a great black bird. His face was visible. His eyes held tears.

Then his features vanished. The hand withdrew.

The hollow voice returned.

“Find me.”

The raven closed its eyes, and the shadow began to remember the shape of the man it had once belonged to.
