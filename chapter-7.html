The Chronicles of Renn — The Raven That Cast No Shadow

Chapter 7 — A Bond That Never Ended

By Lucas Montenegro

Seris's study had become a battlefield of paper. Maps covered the walls. Books lay open across the floor, and broken tablets crowded the table.

Lyra sat beside the window sharpening an arrowhead. Baranion arrived with Elrohir, who had not changed out of his patrol clothes.

“I found his name,” Seris said, placing a drawing before them. “Caelen Veyr.”

“Was he a guardian?” Baranion asked.

“Among other things. He maintained the western concealment matrices when Fyor was much smaller.”

“And the raven?”

“His companion. They were bonded for many years.”

Baranion looked at the bird in the drawing. “What happened?”

Seris unfolded a brittle page. “The accounts become unreliable here.”

Caelen had traveled beyond Fyor on an expedition. His raven returned with him, but it had suffered a terrible injury. According to the records, the creature was dying.

Caelen searched for a way to save it. Eventually, he found fragments of an ancient binding method that did not belong to the traditions practiced in Fyor.

“It wasn't an ordinary Companion Ritual,” Seris explained. “The surviving descriptions warn against it.”

“Why?” Lyra asked.

“Because it attempted to interfere with what two living beings could carry between them.”

“Could it heal the raven?” Baranion asked.

“Perhaps. That was Caelen's hope. But the method was incomplete, and he performed it anyway.”

Elrohir folded his arms. “It failed.”

“Not entirely,” Seris replied. “The raven survived.”

Baranion felt a brief surge of relief. “Then what went wrong?”

“Something Caelen drew away from his companion did not disappear. It became entangled with something of his own.”

“His shadow,” Lyra whispered.

Seris nodded. “Not merely the shape sunlight makes. Something that began to behave like a shadow and eventually took a form of its own.”

Baranion remembered the faceless figure. “Then that man isn't Caelen?”

“I don't think so. I believe it's what remained after Caelen lost control.”

“Where is Caelen?”

Seris lowered her eyes. “The records say he died.”

Baranion sat very still. He remembered the pale hand reaching from the water and the whispered apology.

“If Caelen died,” he asked, “why is the raven still alive?”

Seris pointed to a diagram showing two overlapping circles joined by narrow lines.

“The old account calls the raven a vessel. I think the failed ritual left it holding the consequences in place.”

Lyra looked troubled. “So as long as the raven lives, the faceless thing remains trapped?”

“Possibly.”

“And if the raven dies?”

Seris unfolded another fragment. Only one line remained legible.

Should the vessel perish, the remainder shall be released.

No one spoke.

Finally, Elrohir said, “Then we cannot simply destroy the bird.”

Baranion looked up. “Were you going to?”

“No. But I needed to understand the danger.”

Baranion studied the drawing. Had the raven been stubborn like Vhar? Had it followed Caelen through the forest because it trusted him? How long had it been waiting beside that pool?

Seris found one more account written after the sanctuary was abandoned.

“The keeper refused separation. The companion endured, though neither returned whole.”

She paused.

“The path was concealed, and the place sealed, so that the remainder might never reach the settlement.”

Lyra frowned. “They hid the sanctuary.”

“Yes.”

“But left the raven there.”

Seris said nothing.

Baranion turned to his father. “Was that right?”

“I don't know what choices those people had,” Elrohir said.

“They could have tried to help.”

“Perhaps they did.”

“Then why isn't that written down?”

Elrohir looked at the damaged records. “Sometimes people are more eager to hide a failure than understand it.”

Seris carefully folded the page. “This time, the records will remain.”

They discussed returning to the sanctuary. Seris believed the old markings might still allow the bond to be interrupted, but she could not guarantee the outcome.

“The site is unstable,” she warned. “We could make things worse.”

“Can we rescue the raven?” Baranion asked.

“I don't know.”

“Can we free it?”

“I don't know that either.”

Baranion hated those answers, yet trusted her more for giving them.

Elrohir moved toward the window. “We'll go with a proper patrol. No one acts alone.”

Baranion stood. “I want to come.”

“No.”

“Father—”

“You were nearly killed.”

“I know. But the raven knows me. It let me carry it. It followed me.”

“That doesn't make you safe.”

“No,” Baranion admitted. “But maybe I can help.”

His father watched him.

“I don't want to become its keeper,” Baranion continued. “I don't want to force it to do anything. I just don't want it to be alone again.”

Elrohir looked away. Outside, Vhar settled on the railing.

Finally, Elrohir spoke. “You stay where I can see you. You obey every order. If I tell you to leave, you leave.”

“No arguments,” Baranion promised.

That night, Baranion sat in the storage room beside the empty crate. He had imagined his first companion would make him brave and worthy of becoming a guardian. The injured raven had taught him something different.

A creature could trust him without belonging to him.

And helping it might mean accepting a choice he did not like.

Outside, the forest darkened. Somewhere beyond the western boundary, a silver-eyed raven waited beside a pool reflecting a man who was no longer there.

Tomorrow, Baranion would return.

He only hoped he was not too late.
